Every Graph on at Most Ten Vertices Is Representable by Two 2-Uniform Words

Graphs and Double Occurrence Words

  • Two 2-uniform words represent a graph when a pair of vertices is an edge exactly when the words have equal projections onto that pair
  • All 12,293,434 isomorphism classes of graphs on at most ten vertices lie in G2, each with a pair of words verified by a solver-free checker
  • The counting bound is sharp at 35 vertices, so every minimal forbidden induced subgraph has between 11 and 35 vertices
  • At 36 vertices at most one graph in 5,600 is representable, and at 38 vertices at most one in 10^12

Can Weight-Space Programming Lead to Better Outputs in Medical AI?

Weight-Space Programming for Medical AI

  • Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct reads "denies fever" as "not mentioned" on 78% of denied symptoms in a synthetic symptom-status task
  • One closed-form rank-one change to the layer-20 MLP output projection corrects most of the error, with no gradient computed
  • On 200 held-out notes, cell accuracy rose from 0.656 to 0.879; the denied-as-not-mentioned rate fell from 0.782 to 0.262
  • A repetition penalty, a rules prompt, and activation steering at the same layer gained at most 0.063; the edit rolls back to a residual of 6e-11

CORAL: A Permissioned Ledger Framework for Jointly Signed Negotiated Rates Between Providers and Payers

Jointly Signed Price Transparency Records

  • Hospitals and insurers each publish negotiated prices in separate files; a 2026 study found both sides published the same price for only about 10% of services
  • CORAL is a permissioned ledger on which the provider and the insurer both sign each negotiated price
  • Each published file is checked against the records; a price that differs marks the file that departs
  • Signed and committed one provider's full public file, and compared five providers' files with one national payer's file

ICD-10 Codes from Visit Dialogues

A Framework for Extracting ICD-10 Codes from Visit Dialogues with a System One Model

  • Assigns one ICD-10-CM code to a doctor-patient dialogue with TypeSafe Jev, a System One model that writes no text and gives a probability for each option in a list
  • Walks down the code tree and keeps the 5 best paths; local steps add candidate codes with no model request; one final question selects the code
  • No task training and no labeled example in the model input
  • On 996 held-out MedSynth dialogues: exact code for 62.0% from the 2,032 benchmark codes and 57.3% from all 74,719 codes; a trained text classifier gives 34.3%
  • With a probability threshold of 0.9, the method accepts 59.2% of the dialogues, and 79.3% of those answers are exact
